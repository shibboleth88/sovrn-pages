Writing on CENTS

Essays 17

Ordered as a reading path rather than by date: what the work is, who made it, what it means, then the object itself, then the arguments about its value.

  1. 01
    CENTS & digital objecthoodshibboleth

    How a discrepancy between what a penny is worth and what its copper is worth becomes a work about objecthood itself — from conceptual art’s revolt against the object, through digitisation stripping objects of singularity, to the blockchain giving it back. Sets out the five natures each cent holds at once and the five transformations of value it passes through.

  2. 02
    Rutherford Chang’s Conceptual CollectingMartin Lehnen

    The artist’s method, applied twice. Rule-based collecting without curation, in We Buy White Albums and again in CENTS: mass-produced things kept until time makes them singular, and what changes when the archive moves on-chain.

  3. 03
    CENTS as an Artistic Expression of Bitcoin’s Core IdeasMartin Lehnen

    Why it had to be Bitcoin. Reads the work as a conceptual mirror of the protocol — value, provenance, immutability, trust — and turns up a detail from the earliest Bitcoin source code, where the smallest unit was called a cent rather than a satoshi.

  4. 04
    Materiality and Signification: Rutherford Chang’s CENTS and SemioticsSteven Reiss

    A semiotic reading through Saussure, Peirce and Barthes. The penny is a sign whose meaning was fixed by convention and is unfixed by the artist; its traits are indexical traces — wear, patina, scratches — rather than attributes assigned in advance, which is precisely what separates CENTS from a generative collection.

  5. 05
    on CENTS, white albums, and collecting as art practiceSHL0MS

    The thread that introduced Chang to the crypto audience, written days after the mint. Places the work in a lineage of collection-as-readymade — Feldmann, Boltanski, Song Dong — and tells the story of asking who the artist was and finding out.

  6. 06
    CENTS as a Store of Value. A Thesisbecome.eth

    The investment case, written a week after the mint: why ordinal collections were early against their Ethereum counterparts, why a collector base drawn from both chains matters, and why the author expects this particular gesture to hold its meaning.

  7. 07
    CENTS & M. Spalter’s view of iconic artworkshibboleth

    Michael Spalter’s criteria for what makes an artwork iconic, applied to this one: resonance with an era, universal themes, and earliness in a medium whose defining works are not yet settled.

  8. 08
    The Enduring Echo: Rutherford Chang’s CENTS and the Slow-Burn Legacy of the Avant-GardeCryptoScalpel

    On being understood late. Sets the work beside the avant-garde’s habit of arriving before its audience, and reads the copper block as an inverse economy: money that can no longer buy anything and therefore never stops speaking about value.

  9. 09
    From Blue to Molten CopperMartin Lehnen

    Yves Klein and Rutherford Chang, sixty years apart, both selling something that cannot be held. Klein’s Zones of Immaterial Pictorial Sensibility read against an image inscribed on a satoshi.

  10. 10
    Thoughts about the unique nature of CENTS traits and their significanceMartin Lehnen

    Where the traits come from. Not an algorithm assigning attributes but wear, patina and handling — time-based generative art — and why mint year works as a trait in a way no generative collection can imitate.

  11. 11
    Lustrous CENTS – a Conceptual Contradiction?Martin Lehnen

    Numismatics grades a coin by how little has happened to it. CENTS values exactly what happened to it. What the Sheldon scale and this project each mean by a good coin, and what an untouched, lustrous cent is doing inside a work about wear.

  12. 12
    CENTS – Reflections on Mint Years and their Long-Term SignificanceMartin Lehnen

    The year distribution, counted. Half the collection was struck before 1976 and a quarter between 1980 and 1982; under 3% predates 1960. Sorts the years into the rare, the meme — 1969 — and the conceptual, which is where 1971 comes in.

  13. 13
    Tracing back Chang’s alchemyOffchain_Wizard

    Reads the patina through Wuxing, the five Chinese transformations, matching verdigris, obsidian and the rest to wood, water and the others — on the argument that these traits were made by chemistry, entropy and time rather than by anyone’s decision.

  14. 14
    CENTS: An Exploration of Value on the Bitcoin Blockchainschnörres

    A short account of the project for a Bitcoin readership — the gap between one cent and three cents of copper, the 68-pound block, the ten thousand inscriptions — with a note of where the market stood in the summer after the mint.

  15. 15
    Why Every Collector of Digital Art Must Have CENTS by Rutherford ChangCryptoScalpel

    An argument for the work as a necessary holding rather than a trophy, by way of Lucy Lippard, Walter Benjamin and Kevin McCoy: the smallest unit of currency as the natural bridge between numismatics and the ledger.

  16. 16
    Some thoughts on life, our monetary fiat system, bitcoin, art and a betschnörres

    The fiat reading, argued personally. Inflation, the Cantillon effect, and a penny that costs more to make than it is worth — CENTS as a commentary on a currency dissolving while the object it was stamped into survives.

  17. 17
    Art on Bitcoin Feature #1: From Copper to Email to MilestonesMilestonesNFT

    CENTS in company: the first of a series on conceptual art made on Bitcoin, and clear on a point that still gets mistaken — nothing here is generative or AI, the ten thousand were culled by hand from a hundred thousand.

Press 12

Coverage of the work and of the retrospective it sits in.

  1. From 10,000 pennies to a Beatles record haul, the obsessive work of Rutherford Chang heads to BeijingSimon Bainbridge · The Art Newspaper

    Preview of the exhibition, on the ‘near frightening rigour’ of a post-conceptualist: CENTS and We Buy White Albums among six works, a year after the artist’s death.

  2. Rutherford Chang’s Art of AccumulationMichael Barron · Frieze

    Review of the first institutional retrospective, which opens not with the pennies but with a letter Chang wrote to Nintendo Power about his Tetris high score, and the 2,139 videos that became a work.

  3. After His Untimely Death, Rutherford Chang’s Survey Rewrites What a Square Can DoEmily Watlington · Art in America

    Reads the whole practice through the square — Malevich, Reinhardt, Albers — and then through Chang’s, which are insistently social rather than abstract: the marked-up album sleeves, the ten thousand pennies, a year of Wall Street Journal portraits, and Tetris played relentlessly as a parody of optimisation.

  4. Rutherford Chang Retrospective: Hundreds and Thousands at UCCA BeijingSteven Reiss · Bitcoin Magazine

    On what the usual words — obsessive, conceptual, minimalist — leave out: Chang worked at the exact moment when the promise of sameness fails and handling turns identical objects into singular ones.

  5. Geschichten in Kupfer und VinylTabatha Arlt · monopol

    German-language review of the retrospective, on things one barely looks at — records, coins, game consoles — becoming carriers of time, memory and quiet deviation.

  6. Christie’s Announces CENTS Flagship Ordinal Auctionlorepunk · nft now

    The announcement that Block 839969 — the 4 MB model of the copper ingot, inscribed across a whole Bitcoin block — would go to Christie’s Beyond the Screen, with the block itself shown in New York alongside it.

  7. 收藏、重复与文化记忆丨“张能傑：成百上千”正在UCCA展出UCCA · Tencent News

    UCCA’s own long account of the exhibition, with a documentary film: We Buy White Albums and CENTS set out work by work, and the copper cube given as 31 kg.

  8. 一展带你看懂观念艺术，张能傑回顾展“成百上千”UCCA开幕Beijing Daily

    Opening-day report in Chinese, explaining conceptual art to a general readership through three works — the albums, the cents, and 2,000 recordings of Game Boy Tetris.

  9. The 21st Century’s Biggest Art Trend is Not a Style. But Once You See It, You’ll Notice It Everywhere.Emily Watlington · ArtNews

    The tradition CENTS belongs to, named: systems art, Jack Burnham’s term from 1968, running through Hans Haacke and Adrian Piper to a present full of work trying to make invisible systems legible. A section on Chang argues that his squares and cubes are the opposite of neutral — and that the artist who made them spent his working life inside the financial and logistical systems he was picturing.

  10. Rutherford Chang: Hundreds and Thousandse-flux

    The exhibition announcement: dates, address, and the installation of CENTS #1–10,000, Block 839969 and the CENTS book at UCCA.

  11. Rutherford Chang: Hundreds and ThousandsRichard Bright · Interalia

    The exhibition set out work by work, with the materials named: 3,555 records and a neon sign, a 31-kilogram copper cube, 2,139 Tetris videos and the letters Chang wrote to Nintendo Power. It also carries the fact the copper block now outlives — the US Mint stopped making the penny in November 2025.

  12. Rutherford ChangOcula

    The artist’s profile page: biography and the exhibition record, useful for placing CENTS within a twenty-year practice.
