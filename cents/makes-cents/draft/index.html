Writing on CENTS

Essays, interviews and press on Rutherford Chang’s CENTS. The essays are ordered as a way in rather than by date — read from the top and the project assembles itself; search if you are after one thing.

Searches what is on this page — titles, authors, summaries and topics — not the full text of the pieces themselves.

Essays 17

Ordered as a reading path rather than by date: what the work is, who made it, what it means, then the object itself, then the arguments about its value.

  1. 01
    CENTS & digital objecthoodshibboleth · October 2025

    Start here. How a discrepancy between what a penny is worth and what its copper is worth becomes a work about objecthood itself — from conceptual art’s revolt against the object, through digitisation stripping objects of singularity, to the blockchain giving it back. Sets out the five natures each cent holds at once and the five transformations of value it passes through.

  2. 02
    Rutherford Chang’s Conceptual CollectingMartin Lehnen · March 2025

    The artist’s method, applied twice. Rule-based collecting without curation, in We Buy White Albums and again in CENTS: mass-produced things kept until time makes them singular, and what changes when the archive moves on-chain.

  3. 03
    CENTS as an Artistic Expression of Bitcoin’s Core IdeasMartin Lehnen · March 2025

    Why it had to be Bitcoin. Reads the work as a conceptual mirror of the protocol — value, provenance, immutability, trust — and turns up a detail from the earliest Bitcoin source code, where the smallest unit was called a cent rather than a satoshi.

  4. 04
    Materiality and Signification: Rutherford Chang’s CENTS and SemioticsSteven Reiss · August 2025

    A semiotic reading through Saussure, Peirce and Barthes. The penny is a sign whose meaning was fixed by convention and is unfixed by the artist; its traits are indexical traces — wear, patina, scratches — rather than attributes assigned in advance, which is precisely what separates CENTS from a generative collection.

  5. 05
    on CENTS, white albums, and collecting as art practiceSHL0MS · March 2024

    The thread that introduced Chang to the crypto audience, written days after the mint. Places the work in a lineage of collection-as-readymade — Feldmann, Boltanski, Song Dong — and tells the story of asking who the artist was and finding out.

  6. 06
    CENTS & M. Spalter’s view of iconic artworkshibboleth · April 2025

    Michael Spalter’s criteria for what makes an artwork iconic, applied to this one: resonance with an era, universal themes, and earliness in a medium whose defining works are not yet settled.

  7. 07
    The Enduring Echo: Rutherford Chang’s CENTS and the Slow-Burn Legacy of the Avant-GardeCryptoScalpel · October 2025

    On being understood late. Sets the work beside the avant-garde’s habit of arriving before its audience, and reads the copper block as an inverse economy: money that can no longer buy anything and therefore never stops speaking about value.

  8. 08
    From Blue to Molten CopperMartin Lehnen · March 2025

    Yves Klein and Rutherford Chang, sixty years apart, both selling something that cannot be held. Klein’s Zones of Immaterial Pictorial Sensibility read against an image inscribed on a satoshi.

  9. 09
    Thoughts about the unique nature of CENTS traits and their significanceMartin Lehnen · February 2025

    Where the traits come from. Not an algorithm assigning attributes but wear, patina and handling — time-based generative art — and why mint year works as a trait in a way no generative collection can imitate.

  10. 10
    Lustrous CENTS – a Conceptual Contradiction?Martin Lehnen · February 2025

    Numismatics grades a coin by how little has happened to it. CENTS values exactly what happened to it. What the Sheldon scale and this project each mean by a good coin, and what an untouched, lustrous cent is doing inside a work about wear.

  11. 11
    CENTS – Reflections on Mint Years and their Long-Term SignificanceMartin Lehnen · March 2025

    The year distribution, counted. Half the collection was struck before 1976 and a quarter between 1980 and 1982; under 3% predates 1960. Sorts the years into the rare, the meme — 1969 — and the conceptual, which is where 1971 comes in.

  12. 12
    Tracing back Chang’s alchemyOffchain_Wizard · March 2026

    Reads the patina through Wuxing, the five Chinese transformations, matching verdigris, obsidian and the rest to wood, water and the others — on the argument that these traits were made by chemistry, entropy and time rather than by anyone’s decision.

  13. 13
    CENTS: An Exploration of Value on the Bitcoin Blockchainschnörres · July 2024

    A short account of the project for a Bitcoin readership — the gap between one cent and three cents of copper, the 68-pound block, the ten thousand inscriptions — with a note of where the market stood in the summer after the mint.

  14. 14
    CENTS as a Store of Value. A Thesisbecome.eth · April 2024

    The investment case, written a week after the mint: why ordinal collections were early against their Ethereum counterparts, why a collector base drawn from both chains matters, and why the author expects this particular gesture to hold its meaning.

  15. 15
    Why Every Collector of Digital Art Must Have CENTS by Rutherford ChangCryptoScalpel · September 2025

    An argument for the work as a necessary holding rather than a trophy, by way of Lucy Lippard, Walter Benjamin and Kevin McCoy: the smallest unit of currency as the natural bridge between numismatics and the ledger.

  16. 16
    Some thoughts on life, our monetary fiat system, bitcoin, art and a betschnörres · February 2025

    The fiat reading, argued personally. Inflation, the Cantillon effect, and a penny that costs more to make than it is worth — CENTS as a commentary on a currency dissolving while the object it was stamped into survives.

  17. 17
    Art on Bitcoin Feature #1: From Copper to Email to MilestonesMilestonesNFT · July 2024

    CENTS in company: the first of a series on conceptual art made on Bitcoin, and clear on a point that still gets mistaken — nothing here is generative or AI, the ten thousand were culled by hand from a hundred thousand.
